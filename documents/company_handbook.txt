COMPANY EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK

1. LEAVE AND TIME OFF

Employees receive 18 days of annual paid leave for each calendar year.

Employees may carry forward up to 5 unused annual leave days into the following year. Any unused days above the 5-day carry-forward limit will normally expire at the end of the year.

Employees should submit planned leave requests at least 3 working days before the intended start date.

For emergencies or unexpected situations, employees should inform their manager as soon as reasonably possible.

Employees who are sick should follow the company's sick-leave notification procedure and inform their manager according to their team's requirements.

Parental leave is available to eligible employees according to applicable company policy and local employment regulations.

Managers are responsible for reviewing leave requests and ensuring that team coverage is maintained.

Employees should check their leave balance before submitting extended leave requests.

2. WORKING HOURS AND ATTENDANCE

The standard working schedule is Monday through Friday.

Employees are expected to be available during their team's agreed core working hours.

Employees who expect to arrive late or be unavailable during working hours should inform their manager as early as possible.

Repeated unexplained absences may be referred to the employee's manager and Human Resources for review.

Employees working across different time zones should agree on communication and availability expectations with their manager.

3. REMOTE WORK

Eligible employees may work remotely according to their team's remote-work arrangements.

Remote employees are responsible for maintaining a suitable and secure working environment.

Employees must protect company information when working from home, hotels, cafes, airports, or other public locations.

Employees should avoid discussing confidential company information in public places where conversations may be overheard.

Company laptops should not be left unattended in public locations.

Employees using public Wi-Fi should use the company's approved security controls, such as a corporate VPN, when required.

Remote-work arrangements may be reviewed by managers depending on business requirements.

4. COMPANY LAPTOPS AND IT EQUIPMENT

Employees who require access to internal company systems must use company-issued laptops unless an approved exception exists.

Employees must keep company laptops and other company-owned equipment secure.

Employees must not share their company laptop with unauthorized individuals.

Employees should lock their computer whenever they leave it unattended.

Employees should report lost or stolen company equipment to the IT support team immediately.

If a company laptop is stolen while travelling, the employee should report the incident to IT support and their manager as soon as possible.

Employees must return company-owned equipment when their employment ends or when requested by the company.

5. SOFTWARE INSTALLATION

Employees must not install unauthorized software on company-owned devices.

Software required for business purposes should be requested through the approved IT support process.

Employees should not bypass company security controls to install applications.

Open-source software may only be installed when it is permitted by company policy and approved processes.

Employees should contact IT support if they are unsure whether a particular application is permitted.

6. PASSWORDS AND ACCOUNT SECURITY

Employees must keep their company passwords confidential.

Passwords must not be shared with colleagues, managers, friends, family members, or external parties.

Employees should use strong and unique passwords for company accounts.

Multi-factor authentication should be enabled wherever the company requires it.

Employees should not approve unexpected authentication requests.

If an employee believes that their password has been compromised, they should change the password immediately and contact IT security.

Employees should not write passwords on notes attached to their laptop or monitor.

7. INFORMATION SECURITY

Employees must protect confidential company information from unauthorized access.

Confidential documents should only be shared with people who have a legitimate business need to access them.

Employees should verify unexpected requests for sensitive information before responding.

Security-related incidents should be reported immediately to the IT security team.

Examples of security incidents include suspected phishing attacks, stolen credentials, unauthorized access, malware infections, and lost devices containing company information.

Employees should not attempt to investigate or remove suspected malware themselves unless instructed by the IT security team.

If an employee receives a suspicious email requesting a password or financial information, they should not provide the requested information and should report the message through the company's security reporting process.

8. DATA PRIVACY

Employees must handle personal information responsibly.

Personal information should only be collected, accessed, used, or shared for legitimate business purposes.

Employees should avoid downloading personal information to unauthorized devices or storage locations.

Personal information should not be sent to external parties unless the employee has appropriate authorization.

Any suspected loss or unauthorized disclosure of personal information should be reported to the appropriate privacy or security team.

Employees should follow company retention and deletion requirements for personal information.

9. TRAVEL AND EXPENSES

Employees travelling for approved business purposes may claim eligible business expenses according to the company's expense policy.

Employees should retain receipts for reimbursable expenses.

Travel bookings should normally be made through the company's approved travel process.

Employees should obtain the required approval before making significant business travel arrangements.

Personal expenses should not be submitted as business expenses.

Expense claims should be submitted within the timeframe specified by the company's finance policy.

Managers are responsible for reviewing and approving eligible expenses submitted by their team members.

10. PERFORMANCE REVIEWS

Employees participate in periodic performance reviews.

Performance discussions may include progress against objectives, technical skills, teamwork, communication, and professional development.

Employees should discuss unclear expectations with their manager before the formal review where possible.

Managers should provide employees with feedback throughout the year rather than waiting until the formal review.

Employees may create development goals together with their manager.

Performance review records should be handled confidentially.

11. PROMOTIONS AND CAREER DEVELOPMENT

Promotion decisions consider factors such as sustained performance, demonstrated skills, responsibilities, business requirements, and available roles.

Employees interested in career development should discuss their goals with their manager.

Employees may request opportunities to develop skills through training, mentoring, projects, or increased responsibilities.

Completing a training course does not automatically guarantee promotion.

Employees should maintain an up-to-date record of relevant skills and professional achievements.

12. TRAINING AND LEARNING

The company encourages employees to develop technical and professional skills.

Employees may request relevant training through their manager or the approved learning process.

Training requests should explain how the proposed training supports the employee's current responsibilities or development goals.

Employees participating in company-funded training may be required to follow additional approval or reimbursement rules.

Employees should share useful knowledge with their teams when appropriate.

13. WORKPLACE CONDUCT

Employees are expected to treat colleagues, customers, partners, and other individuals respectfully.

Bullying, harassment, discrimination, intimidation, and threatening behavior are not acceptable.

Employees should raise workplace concerns through their manager, Human Resources, or another approved reporting channel.

Employees should maintain professional behavior in both physical and online workplace environments.

Company communication systems should not be used for unlawful or abusive activity.

14. BUSINESS CONTINUITY AND EMERGENCIES

Employees should follow instructions from the company during major operational disruptions.

If an office becomes unavailable because of an emergency, employees should follow instructions from their manager or the business continuity team.

Employees should keep their emergency contact information up to date where required.

For urgent safety issues, employees should follow local emergency procedures and contact the appropriate emergency services when necessary.

Security incidents should be reported to the IT security team even when they occur outside normal office hours.

15. IT SUPPORT

Employees should submit routine technical problems through the company's IT support system.

Examples include problems with laptops, approved software, network access, printers, and company applications.

Employees should provide enough information for IT support to understand the problem, including relevant error messages and when the issue started.

Employees should not attempt unauthorized changes to company infrastructure.

Critical security incidents should be reported through the designated security reporting channel rather than waiting for a routine IT support response.

16. EMPLOYEE BENEFITS

Eligible employees may receive benefits according to their employment terms and applicable company policies.

Benefits may include health coverage, retirement contributions, employee assistance programs, or other company-sponsored programs depending on eligibility.

Employees should contact Human Resources when they need clarification about benefit eligibility.

Changes in personal circumstances that affect benefits should be reported according to the company's procedures.

17. GENERAL POLICY QUESTIONS

Employees should consult the relevant company policy or contact the appropriate department when they are unsure about a company procedure.

Managers and Human Resources can provide guidance on employment-related policies.

IT support can provide assistance with technical problems.

The IT security team handles security incidents and suspected cybersecurity threats.

The privacy team handles questions and incidents involving personal information.

The finance team handles questions about expenses and reimbursement.

Employees are responsible for following the latest version of applicable company policies.
